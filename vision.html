Roadmap

Pour the foundation. Then scale it.

From a PE on the deck to an AI layer for the whole industry — four phases, each earned by the one before it.

Now · 2025–2026 · As a PE

Pour the foundation.

Right now I'm a project engineer on the $2B LACC CUP build — and that's where the foundation gets poured. Not just concrete, but proof. Every custom tool I make gets its first run on this jobsite, and if it survives a Monday morning at Section A, it has earned the right to go further.

  • Concrete pour tracker, live on the deck
  • Const-Agent across Discord, Teams, Web UI
  • Dashboards + Formwork for the team
  • Credibility banked, one pour at a time
Phase 01 · 1–2 years · Director of AI

Earn the seat. Learn the company.

Step into a Director of AI role — or a seat under Webcor's technology arm — to build trust and learn how the company actually runs. Go cross-project and into the field at Parnassus, GCON, even Obayashi, to see both sides. Then use AI to bridge the field and the technology arm, lifting efficiency on every project we touch.

  • Cross-project field time: Parnassus · GCON · Obayashi
  • Bridge the field ↔ the technology arm with AI
  • Clean up our data — never scammed by a vendor again
  • Harden cybersecurity by mastering the CMiC database
Phase 02 · The AI arm · Equity

Stand up the Webcor AI arm.

With results proven, ask to lead a dedicated AI branch of Webcor — a separate arm, with equity. Inside it we build the Webcor bubble: a contained, self-improving AI ecosystem, proven end-to-end inside a single GC before it goes anywhere else.

  • A separate AI branch — with equity
  • The "Webcor bubble," proven inside one GC
  • Custom-built solutions + Claude where it fits
  • A repeatable playbook, not a one-off
Phase 03 · Expand outward

GCON → Obayashi → the industry.

Once the bubble holds, expand it — first to GCON, then up to our parent Obayashi, then to the rest of the construction industry. The whole point isn't a moonshot. It's survival, and stable, compounding growth — built on a foundation already poured.

  • GCON first — the nearest neighbor
  • Then Obayashi, the parent company
  • Then the broader construction industry
  • Thesis: survival + stable growth
Process

How I build.

Six steps, one loop. Field first, polish last — then do it again.

01

Work hands-on in the field.

Find what really needs improving. If a foreman can't use it with one hand and no English, it doesn't work.

02

Design around what we have.

Design the framework from our existing systems — whatever makes the most sense.

03

Build a core function.

Create the one core function, then test it in the field.

04

Test in the field. Fix bugs.

See if it works on site. Fix what breaks.

05

Make it sharp.

Build a cool, modern front end and add the features that make it shine.

06

Repeat and scale.

Then do it all again — and scale.

Why now

AI is only just becoming AI.

For most of the last few years, "AI" meant a large language model doing very confident autocomplete — pattern-matching its way to an answer and hoping it landed. That era is ending. The frontier is moving from models that retrieve to models that reason, and we're standing at the very front of that curve.

Until now

Autocomplete in a trench coat

Base LLMs predict the next token. Impressive, but brittle — they can't tell you when they're wrong, and they fold on anything that needs real multi-step thought. Most "AI features" shipping today are still just this.

trending_flat
Starting now

Models that actually reason

A new class — trained with reinforcement learning to think before they answer, spend compute at inference time, plan, check their own work, and back up when they're wrong. o1/o3, DeepSeek-R1, extended-thinking Claude: the first generation that reasons instead of recites.

This is the inflection — slowly but surely, AI is crossing from a party trick into something that can carry real weight on a real project. Most of the industry is still bolting last year's autocomplete onto a dashboard. I've spent the journey on the other path: in the field every day, grounded in the actual machine learning instead of just the API, and shipping reasoning-driven tools that already hold up on a $2B job. That's why the time is now — and why I'm ready to be Webcor's AI director.
